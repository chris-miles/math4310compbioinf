## Core question

How do newer sequence technologies and learned representations change the problems we can solve?

::: {.callout-note title="Draft status"}
This lesson is a scaffold.
:::

## Long reads and genome graphs

- Compare short-read and long-read observations of a repeated region.
- Examine which ambiguities additional sequence context resolves and which error assumptions change.
- Introduce pangenome graphs as representations of variation across genomes.

## Protein families and learned representations

- Connect profile HMMs to the hidden-state models studied earlier.
- Compare an explicit sequence model with a learned representation of protein sequences.
- Identify the data used to train each model and the biological claims its output supports.

## A worked comparison

- Use a small repeat example to compare what short and long reads reveal.
- Examine a sequence-representation result alongside its validation evidence.

## Validation and limitations

- Distinguish increased information in the data from a more flexible model.
- Discuss training overlap, distribution shifts, and independent biological validation.
- Separate a method's computational guarantee from evidence that its biological interpretation is correct.

## Exercises

- Choose one method discussed in class. State its input, output, and one independently checkable claim.
- Identify an assumption shared with a method from an earlier lesson, and explain how the newer method changes it.
